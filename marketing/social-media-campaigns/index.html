Example: Summit Scoops tasting launch

Brief: A fictional local ice cream shop wants 30 completed reservations by Sunday for the following Saturday’s tasting. Its primary audience is nearby customers interested in trying new flavors; that audience is a hypothesis to validate. Instagram is the proposed platform, based on an assumed preference for visual flavor previews. Every post supports the same tasting offer and reservation route.

This is a planning model, not a rule to post daily on every platform. The days show sequence; a real brief would provide exact dates, price, location, and event details.

Link CTR

Link clicks ÷ impressions × 100

Example: 40 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 2%

Click-to-reservation rate

Tracked reservations ÷ link clicks × 100

Example: 8 ÷ 40 × 100 = 20%

Cost per reservation

Ad spend ÷ tracked reservations

Example: $48 ÷ 8 = $6

Count rules: Reach counts unique accounts per post; impressions count displays; views use the reporting platform’s viewing rule. Link clicks count clicks on the campaign destination. Reservations count completed tracked actions once. Likes, comments, shares, and saves are action counts, not unique people. A dash or missing value would mean unavailable, not zero.

Final Application: Your Seven-Day Campaign

Use your class brand or Summit Scoops. Submit a written campaign brief and creative plan here; you can reuse your Module 6 designs. If Mr. D assigns production, use this plan to build the actual files in Canva.

  1. Brief: State the objective, numeric target, deadline, primary audience, evidence or labeled hypothesis, platform and reason, main message, offer, CTA route, and primary KPI.
  2. Seven-day calendar: For each day, name the format, purpose, main message or hook, CTA, and metric. Include a promotional static post, a Story, a 15–30-second Reel/TikTok storyboard, behind-the-scenes content, a clearly labeled UGC-style mockup, a helpful post, and a final CTA post.
  3. Creative detail: Give the static post headline and offer details; the Story message and intended action route; and a three-to-five-shot video storyboard totaling 15–30 seconds. Explain how the visual identity stays consistent.
  4. Read the results: Using the practice dashboard, calculate CTR for A, B, and C; calculate C’s click-to-reservation rate. Identify the reservation leader and one limit of this comparison.
  5. Improve: Propose one A/B test. State the variable, two versions, what stays comparable, success metric, and when you would review it. Explain one destination or tracking check before scaling.

Sources and measurement notes

Reviewed October 4, 2026. These official references support the calculation and test concepts. The calendar, dashboard, brands, and teaching scenarios are original classroom examples. Dashboard definitions and platform tools can vary.

  • Google Ads Help: Click-through rate — clicks divided by impressions; this module specifically uses link clicks.
  • Google Ads Help: Conversion rate — conversions relative to eligible interactions and counting settings. Our exercises explicitly use one tracked reservation per action and link clicks as the denominator.
  • TikTok Ads Manager: Split-testing variables — one selected variable for a standard split test.

Measurement habit: Always name the objective, source, period, numerator, denominator, and conversion definition before comparing results.
