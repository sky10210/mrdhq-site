What's Your Plan After High School?

Use this reference alongside Module 14 to explore your choices. There is no single best path for everyone.

Start with yourself

On a scale from 1–10, how confident are you about your plan? Which matters most: income, enjoying the work, stability, flexibility, advancement, or work-life balance? What do you still need to find out?

Eight pathways worth comparing

  1. Work immediately: Earn now, but ask about training, raises, benefits and advancement.
  2. Community college: Explore certificates, associate degrees and transfer programs; check whether credits satisfy your intended major.
  3. Four-year university: Compare net price after grants, not just sticker tuition; add housing, food and fees.
  4. Trade or technical school: Check program cost, accreditation, licensing requirements and job placement.
  5. Paid apprenticeship: Combine work and structured training; programs exist beyond construction.
  6. Military: Explore technical roles and education benefits alongside eligibility, contracts and service obligations.
  7. Entrepreneurship: Test a small business idea and remember revenue is not profit.
  8. Other options: Explore service programs, seasonal jobs, transportation careers, employer-funded education and structured gap years.

Unions: Labor unions represent workers in collective bargaining and sometimes sponsor apprenticeships; a union is not itself a career.

Money questions that change the decision

  • What is the total cost after grants, fees and living expenses?
  • How much will you earn during training and after completing it?
  • What are the benefits, job outlook and opportunities for advancement?
  • Does this pathway require a license, certificate, degree or service commitment?

Career Outlook — BLS research activity
