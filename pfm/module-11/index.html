Choose your vehicle situation

The exact same driver can make different insurance choices depending on whether the car is financed or fully owned.

Why this matters: Pennsylvania law does not require collision or comprehensive just because you own a vehicle. However, a lender can require them while it has a financial interest in a financed car.
2

Start with Pennsylvania-required coverages

These are the protections a standard Pennsylvania personal auto policy must include at minimum. Higher limits cost more but move more financial risk to the insurer.

Bodily Injury Liability

Required
What it pays: damages when you are legally responsible for injuring another person in a crash, including covered medical and rehabilitation expenses. What it does not pay: damage to your own vehicle. Pennsylvania's minimum is $15,000 for one injured person and $30,000 total for one accident.

Property Damage Liability

Required
What it pays: damage you cause to someone else's vehicle or property. Pennsylvania's minimum is only $5,000. Imagine totaling a newer SUV and you can see why students should think about whether minimum coverage is enough.

Medical Benefits

Required
What it pays: medical bills for you and others covered by your policy after an auto accident, regardless of who caused the crash, subject to policy terms. Pennsylvania requires at least $5,000.

Protect yourself from drivers with too little insurance

Uninsured / Underinsured Motorist Bodily Injury

Optional
UM helps protect you, covered relatives, and passengers for bodily injury caused by an at-fault uninsured driver. UIM applies when the at-fault driver has insurance but not enough to cover the injury claim. In Pennsylvania these coverages do not pay for damage to your vehicle.
5

Protect your own vehicle

Collision

Optional
What it covers: covered damage to your own car from a collision, such as hitting another vehicle, a tree, pole, or guardrail. Deductible: the amount you pay toward a covered claim before the insurer pays its share.

Comprehensive

Optional
What it covers: many non-collision losses such as theft, vandalism, weather, falling objects, fire, and animal strikes. Hitting a deer is a classic comprehensive example.
