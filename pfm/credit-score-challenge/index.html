Chapter 4 · Credit knowledge

What is FICO?

FICO stands for Fair Isaac Corporation. FICO Scores are credit-risk scores commonly ranging from 300–850. Typical category weights are payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, credit history length 15%, new credit 10%, and credit mix 10%. Official FICO guide ↗

The three credit bureaus

Experian maintains credit reports, disputes, freezes and consumer monitoring. Experian ↗

Equifax maintains reports and provides dispute, freeze and monitoring services. Equifax ↗

TransUnion maintains credit files and offers disputes, freezes and monitoring. TransUnion ↗

They are bureaus, not credit unions. FICO creates scoring models; bureaus maintain reports. Different report data and scoring models can produce different scores.

How to check for free

AnnualCreditReport.com ↗ offers free weekly credit reports from the three nationwide bureaus. Reports do not automatically include a credit score.

myFICO: free Equifax-based FICO Score 8 plan; useful for seeing a FICO model. myFICO ↗

Credit Karma: free VantageScores using Equifax and TransUnion data, with monitoring and alerts; not the same as FICO. Credit Karma ↗

Bank apps: some banks and card issuers offer free scores and alerts. Check which score model is used, and whether paid upgrades are optional.
