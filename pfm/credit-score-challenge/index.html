Chapter 3 · Credit literacy

What is FICO, and how do you check your credit?

FICO stands for Fair Isaac Corporation. A FICO Score summarizes credit-report information into a number generally ranging from 300 to 850. Learn the five categories: payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, history length 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%. Read the official FICO explanation ↗

Meet the three credit bureaus

Experian: maintains credit reports and offers consumer credit monitoring and score tools. Experian ↗

Equifax: maintains credit files, handles disputes and freezes, and offers consumer credit products. Equifax ↗

TransUnion: maintains credit files, handles disputes and freezes, and offers monitoring tools. TransUnion ↗

They collect and maintain information; FICO is a scoring company, not a bureau. The information in each bureau report can differ, so scores can differ.

Free reports and scores

AnnualCreditReport.com ↗ is the authorized place to request free weekly reports from all three bureaus. These reports do not automatically include credit scores.

myFICO: free plan provides an Equifax-based FICO Score 8, useful for seeing an actual FICO model. myFICO ↗

Credit Karma: free Equifax and TransUnion VantageScores, credit-monitoring alerts, and educational tools; these are not FICO Scores. Credit Karma ↗

Bank or card app: some banks offer free scores in their apps; check whether the score is FICO or VantageScore. Check privacy and optional paid subscriptions before enrolling.
