What makes an effective first resume?

Use clear job titles, concrete tasks, active verbs, reverse-chronological experience, and honest, relevant skills. A simple one-column resume reads well in common application systems. Skip headshots, graphics, tables, full street addresses, and references available upon request.

Weak:

“Helped people and did stuff at work.”

Better:

“Assisted customers, restocked merchandise, and kept displays organized during weekend shifts.”

Experience

Formal employment isn't necessary. Include babysitting, mowing lawns, helping family, club projects, volunteer shifts, or internships. Describe what you actually did; never invent details.

Bullet examples to adapt truthfully:
  • Organized materials and helped prepare equipment for weekly team practices.
  • Provided childcare for two children and maintained a safe, structured schedule.
  • Assisted with stocking, cleaning, and customer questions during busy shifts.
